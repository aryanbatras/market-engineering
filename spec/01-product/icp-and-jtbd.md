# ICP and jobs to be done

---

## Who pays

**An engineering student or final-year engineer at a tier-2 or tier-3 Indian college, three to twelve months from campus placement.**

Not a working professional refreshing their site. Not a bootcamp. Not an agency.

### Why this group

- **They are the ones who cannot solve it themselves.** An engineer who could build a portfolio builds one and never needs us. This is the only group structurally blocked from the obvious solution.
- **The stakes are immediate and concrete.** Placement season has a deadline. A portfolio that is not ready in two weeks is a portfolio that never gets made.
- **The alternative costs more than ₹83.** A designer charges ₹5,000. A coaching class charges more. We are cheaper than doing nothing and cheaper than every real alternative.
- **They share.** One person publishes, three friends ask how. Distribution is built into the audience.
- **They are early.** Final-year today is a working engineer in two years who still updates their portfolio. That is the retention story.

### Who does not pay

| Group | Why not |
|---|---|
| Working engineers with sites | Already solved it |
| Developers who enjoy front-end | They would rather build it |
| Agencies and freelancers | Need multi-project, client management, white-labelling — a different product |
| Non-engineering students | Out of scope for v1; a natural v2 expansion |
| International users | Razorpay is INR and India-first. Deliberate v2 scope. |

## Jobs to be done

### Job 1 — When placement season starts, help me look like a competent engineer

> "When campus placement opens, I need recruiters to see me as a real engineer, because my college name does not do that work for me."

**This is the primary job.** Everything else serves it.

**Success:** they send the link, and the conversation starts on their skills rather than on their college.

---

### Job 2 — When I sit down to update my portfolio, help me not start from scratch

> "I already have my resume and my GitHub. I do not want to spend a weekend retyping my own life into a form."

**Why this is the most operationally important job.** The cost of our product is entirely in data entry. Anyone can make a template; the hard part is getting the user's content into it.

**Success:** they go from nothing to a complete portfolio in one sitting.

---

### Job 3 — When I choose a template, help me not look generic

> "There are forty of these on the internet and they all look the same. If mine looks like everyone else's, I am invisible again."

**Rohit's job, and the one most likely to convert.** They are technical enough to recognise good design and insecure enough to worry about fitting in. Only a portfolio that does not look like everyone else's resolves both.

**Success:** they pick a template because it looks like them, not because it was the only option.

---

### Job 4 — When a recruiter opens my link, help me make a good first impression

> "A recruiter spends about ten seconds on my page. I need them to understand what I can do in those ten seconds."

**Success:** the recruiter asks about a project. That is the whole metric.

---

### Job 5 — When I apply somewhere, help me hand them something that looks considered

> "They want a PDF. I want it to look like I put effort in, not like I clicked export on a default."

**Success:** they attach it without apologising.

---

### Job 6 — When I want to leave, do not make it hard

> "If this stops being useful I should be able to take my data and go."

**A real job, not a soft one.** Data captivity signals that a free tool might vanish. It is also the fastest route to a refund request and a bad review.

**Success:** they export on the way out and tell other people we were decent about it.

## Competitive position

| Alternative | Their advantage | Our advantage |
|---|---|---|
| **Hand-coded portfolio** | Exactly what they want | Requires the skill they do not have |
| **Wix / WordPress / Canva** | Familiar, powerful | Produces a landing page, not an engineer's portfolio |
| **Free HTML templates** | Free, good looking | Requires knowing what an HTML file is |
| **LinkedIn** | Already exists, recruiters already look | No feedback, no control over presentation |
| **Resume builders** | Good at the document | Nothing about presentation or proof |
| **A designer** | Excellent result | ₹5,000, and weeks |

**Nobody is competing on the actual axis.** Everyone serves people who can already do this. We serve the people everyone else assumes can handle it.

## Willingness to pay

**₹83/month, founder decision.**

The test we are betting on: at that price, a fresher does not consult anyone. They do not need a budget. They do not need to justify it. Below roughly ₹100 the decision stops being a decision.

**What they are buying:** not a portfolio — they can get one free. They are buying a portfolio that looks like it was built by someone who knows what they are doing, and continuing access to templates that keep getting better.

**Free must be genuinely useful.** A deliberately crippled free tier converts nobody. It just produces people who feel tricked and never return.

---

## Where the ICP will change

Not now, but recorded so we notice when it happens.

- **Working engineers** — as the current cohort ages, the ICP drifts upward. Same product, different pitch.
- **Non-engineering students** — same problem, same product, different templates.
- **International** — needs a different payment provider and different templates. v2.
- **Bootcamps and placement cells** — the natural B2B. A placement cell paying for a cohort is a completely different sales motion and pricing. Not v1.
