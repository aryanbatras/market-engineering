# Problem statement

---

## The problem

Roughly 90% of engineering students in tier-2 and tier-3 Indian colleges face two problems at once, and they compound.

**They do not know how to code as much as the market assumes.** Many have never written a line of production code outside a lab exercise. Some are learning. Most do not know where they stand, because nobody has ever told them.

**They do not know how to market what they can do.** This is the half nobody mentions. Marketing is treated as a soft skill that matters later, or as something "you pick up as you network." So an engineer with real ability — and an engineer with none — present identically. Both are invisible.

**The result:** engineers who deserve far more are routinely taken for granted and underpaid. Engineers who were good at one specific thing never get placed, not because of ability but because nobody ever found out they had it.

**Marketing is part of software, and software education barely teaches it.**

---

## Why existing solutions do not reach them

| Existing path | Why it fails for this user |
|---|---|
| **"Build a portfolio" advice** | Arrives at the worst possible moment — after they need it. And they have no time. |
| **Hand-coding a portfolio** | Requires the exact skill they do not have yet. Circular. |
| **Free HTML resume templates** | Technically excellent, and completely inaccessible. Someone must first know what an HTML file is. |
| **Wix / WordPress / Canva** | They will build something. It will be a landing page with a stock hero image and a parallax scroll. It will not survive a recruiter's second glance, and it does not look like an engineer wrote it. |
| **LinkedIn optimisation** | The platform gives no feedback. You cannot tell whether your profile is working until months later, if ever. |
| **Resume builders** | Good at the document. Nothing about the presentation, the framing, or the proof. |
| **Getting "marketing awareness" late** | The awareness arrives with no time left to act on it. |

**The common failure is timing combined with craft.** The user is told to do a hard thing at the hardest possible moment, with no template that already looks credible.

---

## The problem with the problem

The advice "just make a good portfolio" contains two hidden assumptions:

1. **That the user knows what a good portfolio looks like.** They do not. They have never seen ten of them side by side.
2. **That they can build one.** If they could, they would not be here.

So the advice is correct and useless. Someone who could act on it does not need to hear it, and everyone who hears it cannot act on it.

**Our users are exactly the group this advice fails.** That is the gap.

---

## What they actually need

1. **To be seen as an engineer, not a candidate.** The output has to look like a competent engineer made it, because that is part of the signal.
2. **To not start from nothing.** Templates that already look credible, so they bring their content rather than their taste.
3. **To not lose their weekend to data entry.** Their existing content is in a resume PDF, a GitHub profile, or a LinkedIn export. None of it is structured.
4. **To know whether it worked.** Right now they cannot tell whether their profile is any good until months later.
5. **To get it out into the world.** A portfolio that exists on a localhost is worth nothing.

---

## Our answer

A single platform that takes what they already have — scattered, unstructured, in the wrong format — and turns it into a portfolio that looks like a competent engineer built it, in under ten minutes.

**The portfolio generator is the first feature, not the whole product.** It is the wedge: the thing that gets them in, proves the value, and gives us a live URL their recruiter can actually open.

The wider platform — resume tailoring, LinkedIn framing, content, video — follows, on the same account and the same subscription.

---

## Who this is not for

**Engineers who can already build their own portfolio.** They already have one, or they could make one in an evening. They will not visit, and we do not want them to.

**This is stated plainly because it is the product's thesis, not a limitation.** If a technical person has the ability to create a portfolio, they create one and we are irrelevant to them. We are for everyone else.

It also explains the audience skew: our users are, by construction, non-technical. Which drives the design — light backgrounds, large type, generous spacing, nothing that rewards effort to figure out.
